P
Probe

Probe

Ask open-ended, detail-oriented questions to fully understand their situation. You're building a complete picture of their world — business needs, tech stack, objectives, and active initiatives — before you say a word about your product.

What You're Uncovering
🏢
Business Situation
Company goals, growth stage, org structure, key priorities this year
🔧
Tech Stack
Languages, cloud platforms, CI/CD tools, version control, dev tooling
🎯
Tech Objectives
What they're trying to achieve technically — speed, scale, quality, security
🚀
Active Initiatives
Current projects, migrations, hires, transformation programs underway
👥
Team Structure
Engineering headcount, team size, review processes, how work gets done
📏
Metrics & KPIs
How they measure success — velocity, quality, uptime, deployment frequency
Probe Questions
What does your engineering org look like today — how many developers, how are they organized?
Walk me through your current development workflow — from code commit to production. What does that look like?
What languages and cloud platforms are your teams working with?
What are the two or three biggest technical priorities for your team this year?
Are there any major migrations, platform changes, or new product initiatives underway right now?
How do you measure engineering quality and velocity today? What does "good" look like for your team?
Tips for Probing
🎙️
Talk 20%, Listen 80%
Probe is about them, not you. Ask one question, then stop and listen fully before asking another.
🔍
Go Deeper, Not Wider
When they mention a problem or challenge, dig in. "Tell me more about that" is your best follow-up.
📝
Take Notes Visibly
Shows you're paying attention and care about what they say. Their exact words matter — you'll use them in Align.
🚫
Don't Pitch Yet
Resist the urge to respond to pain signals with product features. Save it. Earn the right to pitch.
A
Align

Align

Repeat back what you heard. Get into their shoes. Show that you truly understand their world before moving forward. Alignment builds trust, confirms your understanding, and creates the psychological safety for the prospect to open up more.

The Align Loop
👂
Reflect
Mirror their exact words back. Don't paraphrase — use their language.
→
🤝
Empathize
Acknowledge the weight of what they're dealing with. Validate without exaggerating.
→
✅
Confirm
Check your understanding. "Is that right?" gives them a chance to correct or add more.
Alignment Phrases
So if I'm hearing you correctly — you're scaling the engineering team rapidly, but the code review process isn't keeping up with that growth. Is that right?
That makes total sense. You're under pressure to ship faster, but not at the cost of quality — and right now those two things feel like they're in tension.
It sounds like the DevSecOps mandate came from the top, but the tooling to support it hasn't fully caught up yet. That's a really common challenge for teams at your growth stage.
You mentioned that your senior engineers are spending a disproportionate amount of time on review. If I were in your shoes, I'd want to solve that before it becomes a retention issue.
Just to make sure I've got the full picture — the main friction points are [X, Y, Z]. Did I capture that?
Alignment Do's & Don'ts
✅
Do: Use their words
If they said "our review process is a bottleneck," don't say "code quality issues." Echo their exact framing.
✅
Do: Name the emotion
"That sounds frustrating." Simple acknowledgment signals you're a partner, not just a vendor.
❌
Don't: Invent problems
Only reflect back what they actually said. Adding problems they didn't mention destroys trust.
❌
Don't: Pivot to pitch
Alignment is not your segue to "and that's why our product..." — that comes in Raise, after they're ready.
R
Raise

Raise

Move the prospect up the decision ladder — from unaware to convinced. Point out the flaws in the status quo, highlight the pain, and gently challenge their thinking until they reach the conclusion that they need what you offer. Never push — guide.

🪜 The Decision Ladder

Each rung represents a shift in the prospect's mindset. Your job is to help them climb — one rung at a time.
6
🏆 Advocate
They're sold and selling for you internally. Champion status.
"I've already mentioned this to my CTO — can you put together a one-pager I can share?"
5
✅ Committed to Change
They agree the status quo must change and are actively evaluating options.
"What would a trial look like? I want to show my team."
4
🔍 Actively Seeking a Solution
Pain is real enough that they're now looking for answers. They've moved from passive to active.
"We've actually been looking at a couple of options in this space."
3
😬 Feeling the Pain
They recognize the gap is causing real problems — slowdowns, quality issues, team friction.
"Yeah, our review backlog is definitely slowing us down."
2
🤔 Aware of the Gap
They know something could be better, but haven't felt the urgency yet.
"We've talked about improving our review process but haven't prioritized it."
1
😶 Unaware / Status Quo
No felt need. Current state seems fine to them. Your job: gently surface the gap.
"Our process works. Developers review each other's code."
Raise Tactics
🕳️
Surface the Gap
Ask questions that expose the delta between where they are and where they want to be. Let them feel the distance.
💡
Highlight the Cost
Help them quantify what the status quo is actually costing — in time, quality, engineer hours, or risk.
🤲
Gentle Challenge
Respectfully question assumptions. "Have you thought about what happens when the team doubles?" Not confrontational — curious.
Raise Questions
You mentioned senior engineers spend time on reviews — what's the rough time cost per week across the team?
If your engineering headcount doubles in the next year, does your current review process scale with that?
What happens to your shipping velocity when a key reviewer is out or overloaded?
How much of your DevSecOps mandate is actually enforced at the PR level today, versus aspirational?
If you could get that review time back, what would your team do with it?
What's the cost — to morale, to throughput — if junior engineers aren't getting timely, detailed feedback on their PRs?
The Complete PAR Flow
Each step builds on the last. Skip one and the whole sequence breaks down.

🎯
Probe Goal
Know their stack, org, goals, and initiatives better than they've articulated them before. Be the person who finally asked the right questions.
🪞
Align Goal
Make them feel genuinely heard — not just processed. Trust unlocks honesty. Honesty leads to real pain, real budget, real urgency.
🪜
Raise Goal
They conclude — on their own — that the status quo is costing them and that they need a change. You didn't push them. You guided them.
The Golden Rule of PAR
"The best salespeople don't tell prospects what they need.
They ask questions until the prospect tells them."
